## What to study on each
### 1. Hoyne (Australia)
**URL:** https://hoyne.com.au/  
**Specialization:** Property branding & placemaking agency  
**Why it is on the list:** Best-in-class property brand agency: calm, luxurious case studies of developments with big renders and type.  
**Study / adapt:** Hero: calm, full-bleed development imagery with a single confident line of copy. Navigation: tiny, quiet, lets the work breathe. Section composition: case studies lead with the place brand, then renders, then results. Typography: refined serif/sans pairing that reads as property-grade luxury. Portfolio: development case studies with real outcomes (sales, launches). Footer: simple, contact-first. Adapt: the 'place brand -> campaign -> results' case-study spine.

### 2. The Boundary (UK)
**URL:** https://www.the-boundary.com/  
**Specialization:** Architectural visualisation & film studio  
**Why it is on the list:** Immersive, film-led site for luxury developments.  
**Study / adapt:** Hero: cinematic CGI film on autoplay with minimal overlay. Video interaction: the showreel is the hero and project pages continue the film language. Section composition: services (CGI, film, real-time, digital twins) each get a visual proof. Typography: restrained, lets imagery dominate. Adapt: film-first hero and the way services are proven by media rather than text.

### 3. DBOX (USA)
**URL:** https://dbox.com/  
**Specialization:** Branding & visualisation for real estate  
**Why it is on the list:** Minimal, elegant case studies for landmark developments.  
**Study / adapt:** Hero: single landmark project, large and quiet. Navigation: minimal, text-only. Portfolio: luxury developments (432 Park Ave etc.) presented one at a time with generous white space. Typography: editorial serif with small caps labels. Adapt: the 'one project, full attention' rhythm and luxury restraint.

### 4. Williams New York (USA)
**URL:** https://williamsnewyork.com/

**Why it is on the list:** Premium NYC property marketing; clean, image-led development case studies.

Section composition: service pillars (branding, digital, film, print) tied to property outcomes. Adapt: how a property-marketing agency structures services around a launch lifecycle.
